\begin{abstract}                % Abstract of 50--100 words
   Besides the traditional Lyapunov stability theory, contraction theory offers a interesting perspective on the stability of nonlinear systems.
   It allows us to analyze the stability of systems by examining the convergence of trajectories in the state space.
   In this paper, the contraction theory is applied to the control of synchronous machines, specifically permanent magnet synchronous motors (PMSMs).
   The proposed control strategy is mainly designed to ensure the contraction of the system trajectories to a desired system trajectories, under unknown (and stochastic) disturbances.
   Real time validation of the proposed control strategy is performed on a PMSM system.
   The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed control strategy in achieving stability and robustness in the presence of disturbances.
   
\end{abstract}

\begin{keyword}
   Contraction theory, permanent magnet synchronous motor (PMSM)
\end{keyword}

\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Mathematical Modeling of Synchronous Machines}

Let us consider a synchronous machine presented in \cite{Lee:2018aa}, as follows:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:model:SM}
   \begin{cases}
      \ddtt{\theta}
      =&
      \omega
      \\
      \ddtt{\omega}
      =&
      \tfrac{1}{J}
      \big(
         \tau-f_v\omega+\tau_L
      \big)
      ,
   \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\theta\in\R$ is the rotor angle, $\omega\in\R$ is the rotor speed, $J\in\R_{>0}$ is the moment of inertia, $\tau:=\tau(\ia,\ib)\in\R^2$ is the electromagnetic torque generated by the machine, $f_v\in\R_{>0}$ is the viscous friction coefficient, and $\tau_L\in\R$ is the load torque.
The electromagnetic torque $\tau\in\R$ is typically a function of the phase currents $\ia\in\R$ and $\ib\in\R$ of the machine, which can be expressed as:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:tau:SM}
   \tau
   = 
   -\tfrac{3}{2} P\Phi (\sin(P\theta) \ia + \cos(P\theta) \ib)
   ,
\end{equation}
where $P\in\N$ is the number of pole pairs and $\Phi\in\R$ is the flux linkage.

For brevity, we will denote the system \eqref{eq:model:SM} as general state space form:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:model:SM:state}
   \ddtt\mv{x}
   =
   \mm{A} \mv{x}
   +
   \mm{B}_1 \mv{u}
   +
   \mm{B}_2 d
   ,
\end{equation}
where $\mv{x} := \left(\theta, \omega\right)^\top\in\R^2$, $\mv{u} := \left(\ia, \ib\right)^\top\in\R^2$ and $d := \tau_L$ denote the state vector, the control input vector and the disturbance, respectively.
The system matrix $\mm{A}\in\R^{2\times 2}$ and the input matrices $\mm{B}_1:=\mm{B}_1(\mv{x}):\R^2\to\R^{2\times 2}$ and $\mm{B}_2\in\R^2$ are defined as follows:
\begin{equation}
   \mm{A} =
   \begin{bmatrix}
      0 & 1 \\
      0 & -\tfrac{f_v}{J}
   \end{bmatrix},
   \quad
   \mm{B}_1 =
   \begin{bmatrix}
      0 & 0 \\
      -\tfrac{3P\Phi}{2J}\sin(P\theta) & \tfrac{3P\Phi}{2J}\cos(P\theta)
   \end{bmatrix}
   ,
\end{equation}
and $\mm{B}_2 = \left( 0, 1 \right)^\top$.
It is well known that the system \eqref{eq:model:SM:state} is controllable and observable under the assumption that the system parameters are known \MSRY{..? REF}.

\subsection{Contraction Theory}

\subsubsection{Contraction Metrics} \label{sec:sub:contraction}

Consider that we have the following deterministic nonlinear system:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sys}
   \ddtt\mv{x}
   =
   \mv{f}(\mv{x},t)
   .
\end{equation}
The contraction theory starts with the definition of \textit{differential dynamics} of the system \eqref{eq:sys} using \textit{differential displacement} $\delta\mv{x}$ \cite[Chap 4]{Kirk:2004aa}, which is defined as follows:
\begin{equation}
   \ddtt{\delta\mv{x}}
   =
   \pptfrac{\mv{f}}{\mv{x}} \delta\mv{x}
   .
\end{equation}
The differential displacement $\delta\mv{x}$ is the infinitesimal displacement at \textit{fixed time}.

The contraction property of the system \eqref{eq:sys} can be analyzed using the Lyapunov function $V:=V(\mv{x},t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\mv{x}^\top \mm{M} \delta\mv{x}$, where $\mm{M} = \mm{M}^\top := \mm{M}(\mv{x},t) \succ 0$ denotes the \textit{contraction metric}.
For the system \eqref{eq:sys} to be contracting, the following condition must hold:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:contraction:condition}
   \ddtt\mm{M} 
   + 
   2\mysym(
      \mm{M} \pptfrac{\mv{f}}{\mv{x}}
   ) 
   \leq 
   -2\alpha\mm{M}
   ,
\end{equation}
which leads to $\ddtt V \leq -\alpha V$ for some $\alpha\in\R_{>0}$.
In other words, all solutions of the system \eqref{eq:sys} are contracting to a unique solution with an exponential rate $\alpha$.

On the other hand, the partial contraction theory investigates the contraction of the system using a virtual system.
Following theorem details the partial contraction theory.

\begin{thm} [see \cite{Wang:2004aa}] \label{thm:partial:contraction}
   Consider a nonlinear system of the form 
   $
      \ddtt\mv{x} = \mv{f}(\mv{x},\mv{x},t)
   $
   and the virtual system 
   $
      \ddtt\mv{q} = \mv{f}(\mv{q},\mv{x},t)
   $
   are contracting with respect to $\mv{q}$.
   If a particular solution of virtual system verifies a smooth specific property, then all solutions of the original system verifies this property exponentially.
\end{thm}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Motivated by the use of the partial contraction theory in \cite{Tsukamoto:2021af}, \MSRY{and more...}, we assume that the desired trajectory (including desired control input) of the system is given without disturbances, as follows:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:desired:sys}
   \ddtt\mv{x}^*   =
   \mm{A} \mv{x}^*
   +
   \mm{B}_1 \mv{u}^*
   ,
\end{equation}
where $\mv{x}^*$ is the desired state vector and $\mv{u}^*$ is the desired control input vector.

Finally, the control objective is to design a control input $\mv{u}$ (current references) such that the system trajectories of the system \eqref{eq:desired:sys} and the desired system \eqref{eq:desired:sys} converges.
We assume that a lower lever control is designed to track the current references $\mv{u}$, and thus we can focus on the control design for the system \eqref{eq:model:SM:state}.

\subsection{Control Design}

Following the control design in \cite{Tsukamoto:2021af}, state-dependent coefficient (SDC) formulation \cite{Dani:2015aa} is used to represent the error dynamics \MSRY{more details}.
The SDC $\mathbb{A}:=\mathbb{A}(\mv{x},\mv{x}^*)$ is defined as follows:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:SDC}
   \mathbb{A}
   :=
   \textstyle\int_0^1 
   \left[
      \pptfrac{\mv{f}}{\mv{x}}     
   \right]
   \big(
      c \mv{x} + (1-c) \mv{x}^*
   \big)
   \,
   \der c
   ,
\end{equation}
where 
$
   \mv{f}
   :=
   \mv{f} (\mv{x},\mv{x}^*)
   =
   \mm{A}(\mv{x}-\mv{x}^*)
   + \mm{B}_1 \mv{u} 
   - \mm{B}_1^* \mv{u}^*
$
and $\mm{B}_1^* := \mm{B}_1(\mv{x}^*)$, and $c$ is a dummy variable for the integration.
The SDC for the system \eqref{eq:sys} is derived in Appendix \ref{sec:appendix:SDC} (see \eqref{eq:SDC:explicit:2}).
The existence... blah blah can be found in \cite{Dani:2015aa}, \MSRY{and more...}.

Then, the error dynamics can be expressed as:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:error:dyn}
   \begin{aligned}
      \ddtt
      (
         \mv{x} - \mv{x}^*
      )
      =&
      \mm{A} (
         \mv{x} - \mv{x}^*
      )
      + \mm{B}_1 \mv{u}
      - \mm{B}_1^* \mv{u}^*
      + \mm{B}_2 d
      \\
      =&
      \underbrace
      {
         \mm{A} (
            \mv{x} - \mv{x}^*
         )
         +
         (
            \mm{B}_1 
            - \mm{B}_1^* 
         ) \mv{u}^*
      }_{= \mathbb{A}(\mv{x},\mv{x}^*) (\mv{x} - \mv{x}^*)}
      \\
      &
      - \mm{B}_1 
      (
         \mv{u} - \mv{u}^*
      )
      + \mm{B}_2 d
      \\
      =&
      \mathbb{A} (\mv{x} - \mv{x}^*)
      - \mm{B}_1 
      (\mv{u} - \mv{u}^*)
      + \mm{B}_2 d
      \\
      =&
      \left(
         \mathbb{A} - \mm{B}_1 \mm{R}^{-1} \mm{B}_1 ^\top \mm{M}
      \right)
      (\mv{x} - \mv{x}^*)
      +
      \mm{B}_2 d
      ,
   \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the control input $\mv{u}$ defined as:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:control:input}
   \mv{u}
   =
   \mv{u}^*
   -
   \mm{R}^{-1} \mm{B}_1^\top \mm{M} (\mv{x} - \mv{x}^*)
\end{equation}
where $\mm{R} \succ 0$ is a positive definite matrix and $\mm{M}:=\mm{M}(\mv{x},\mv{x^*},t) \succ 0$ is the contraction metric defined in Section \ref{sec:sub:contraction}.

Finally, invoking the partial contraction theory reviewed in Section \ref{sec:sub:contraction}, we can define the virtual system as follows:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sys:virtual}
   \ddtt\mv{q}
   =
   \ddtt\mv{x}^*
   +
   \left(
      \mathbb{A} - \mm{B}_1\mm{R}^{-1} \mm{B}_1^\top \mm{M}
   \right)
   (\mv{q} - \mv{x}^*)
   +
   \mm{B}_2 d_{\mu}
   ,
\end{equation}
where $\mv{q}$ is the virtual state vector and $d_{\mu}:=d_{\mu}(d,\mu), \forall \mu\in[0,1]$ is defined as $d_{\mu} = d_{\mu}(d,0)$ and $d_{\mu} = d_{\mu}(d,1)$.
The differential dynamics is then defined as:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sys:virtual:variational}
   \ddtt{\delta{\mv{q}}}
   =
   \left(
      \mathbb{A} - \mm{B}_1\mm{R}^{-1} \mm{B}_1^\top \mm{M}
   \right)
   \delta{\mv{q}}
   .
\end{equation}

Then according to Theorem \ref{thm:contraction:perturbed} and the partial contraction theory, the system \eqref{eq:sys:virtual} and the system \eqref{eq:desired:sys} will converge to the desired trajectory $\mv{x}^*$ with steady state error:

(in parentheses). This applies to papers in data storage. For example,
write ``$15\,\mathrm{Gb}/\mathrm{cm}^2$ ($100\,\mathrm{Gb}/\mathrm{in}^2$)''.

units, clearly state the units for each quantity in an equation.  The
SI unit for magnetic field strength $\mathbf{H}$ is $\mathrm{A}/\mathrm{m}$. However, if you wish to
use units of $\mathrm{T}$, either refer to magnetic flux density $\mathbf{B}$ or
magnetic field strength symbolized as $\mu_0\,\mathbf{H}$. Use the center dot to
separate compound units, e.g., ``$\mathrm{A} \cdot \mathrm{m}^2$''.

\subsection{Abbreviations and Acronyms}

Define abbreviations and acronyms the first time they are used in the
text, even after they have already been defined in the
abstract. Abbreviations such as IFAC, SI, ac, and dc do not have to be
defined. Abbreviations that incorporate periods should not have
spaces: write ``C.N.R.S.'', not ``C. N. R. S.'' Do not use abbreviations
in the title unless they are unavoidable (for example, ``IFAC'' in the
title of this article).

\subsection{Equations}

Number equations consecutively with equation numbers in parentheses
flush with the right margin, as in (\ref{eq:sample}).  To make your equations more
compact, you may use the solidus ($/$), the $\exp$ function, or
appropriate exponents. Use parentheses to avoid ambiguities in
denominators. Punctuate equations when they are part of a sentence, as
in

\begin{equation} \label{eq:sample2}

\int_0^{r_2} & F (r, \varphi ) dr d\varphi = [\sigma r_2 / (2 \mu_0 )] \\

\end{array}
\end{equation}

Be sure that the symbols in your equation have been defined before the
equation appears or immediately following. Italicize symbols ($T$
might refer to temperature, but T is the unit tesla). Refer to
``(\ref{eq:sample})'', not ``Eq. (\ref{eq:sample})'' or ``equation
(\ref{eq:sample})'', except at the beginning of a sentence: ``Equation
(\ref{eq:sample}) is \ldots''.

\subsection{Other Recommendations}

Use one space after periods and colons. Hyphenate complex modifiers:
``zero-field-cooled magnetization''. Avoid dangling participles, such
as, ``Using (1), the potential was calculated'' (it is not clear who or
what used (1)). Write instead: ``The potential was calculated by using
(1)'', or ``Using (1), we calculated the potential''.

A parenthetical statement at the end of a sentence is punctuated
outside of the closing parenthesis (like this). (A parenthetical
sentence is punctuated within the parentheses.) Avoid contractions;
for example, write ``do not'' instead of ``don' t''. The serial comma
is preferred: ``A, B, and C'' instead of ``A, B and C''.


\section{Conclusion}

A conclusion section is not required. Although a conclusion may review
the main points of the paper, do not replicate the abstract as the
conclusion. A conclusion might elaborate on the importance of the work
or suggest applications and extensions.

         \pptfrac{}{\mv{x}}
         \left(
            \mm{A} \mv{x}
            +
            \mm{B}_1 \mv{u}^*
         \right)
      \right]
      \big(
         c \mv{x} + (1-c) \mv{x}^*
      \big)
      \,
      \der c
      \\
      =&
      \textstyle\int_0^1

         \left(
            \mm{A}

\end{equation}

$
